\chapter{Introduction}
The necessity for predictable, deterministic execution in agentic systems drives the development of the Chatman Engine v26.7.9. Contemporary AI pipelines often suffer from non-deterministic variance in event logging and artifact generation. We present an architecture--the S1-S6 pipeline--that strictly governs process transitions. 

This thesis formalizes the operational model into rigorous mathematical structures. We define the foundational spaces of Hypothesis ($\Hyp$), Admission ($\Adm$), and Refusal ($\Rfs$). The core objective is to demonstrate that the specification-implementation gap $\delta$ can be forced to zero exclusively through categorical constraints, ensuring that unpermitted execution becomes fundamentally a type-error rather than merely a detected policy violation.

The S1-S6 pipeline operates over six distinct stages: snapshot derivation (RDFC-1.0 hashing), OWL closure computation, PDDL plan synthesis projection, POWL v2 trace admission, knowledge hooks resolution, and 9-hash \texttt{ProcessReceiptEnvelope} generation. Each stage is mapped onto strict algebraic structures bounded by geometric constraints, establishing a fully governed process CPU.
