# Swarm Calculus & Physics: Foundational References

This document compiles the foundational citations and original literature for key concepts in Swarm Calculus & Physics, supporting the planetary-scale continuous limits and stability frameworks of the Bounded Receipted Chatman Equation (BRCE).

---

## 1. Mean-Field Games & Limits

These foundational works establish the mathematical framework for scaling discrete multi-agent games to continuous density fields (mean-field limits), justifying the transition from individual agent operations to macro-scale density operators in Theorem 11.1 (Chatman Mean-Field Convergence).

### Lasry & Lions (2007)
*   **Authors:** Jean-Michel Lasry, Pierre-Louis Lions
*   **Title:** Mean field games
*   **Year:** 2007
*   **Publication Venue:** *Japanese Journal of Mathematics*
*   **Volume, Issue, Pages:** Volume 2, Issue 1, Pages 229–260
*   **DOI:** [10.1007/s11537-007-0657-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11537-007-0657-8)
*   **Abstract/Scope:** This seminal work introduces the mathematical framework of mean field games, which models decision-making processes with a very large number of rational agents. It establishes the coupled system of a backward Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation (for optimal control) and a forward Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck equation (for state density).

### Caines, Huang, & Malhamé (2006)
*   **Authors:** Minyi Huang, Roland P. Malhamé, Peter E. Caines
*   **Title:** Large population stochastic dynamic games: closed-loop McKean-Vlasov systems and the Nash certainty equivalence principle
*   **Year:** 2006
*   **Publication Venue:** *Communications in Information and Systems*
*   **Volume, Issue, Pages:** Volume 6, Issue 3, Pages 221–252
*   **DOI:** [10.4310/CIS.2006.v6.n3.a3](https://dx.doi.org/10.4310/CIS.2006.v6.n3.a3)
*   **Abstract/Scope:** This foundational paper independently develops the Nash Certainty Equivalence (NCE) principle for large-population stochastic dynamic games, showing how decentralized controllers achieve an approximate Nash equilibrium in the infinite-agent limit.

## 2. Consequence Advection-Diffusion

These classic references provide the physical and mathematical basis for the Consequence Transport Equation (advection-diffusion-reaction), describing how uncommitted consequences propagate through a spatial swarm via bulk agent motion (advection) and consensus sharing (diffusion).

### Fick's Laws of Diffusion (1855)
*   **Author:** Adolf Fick
*   **Original Title (German):** Ueber Diffusion
*   **Year:** 1855
*   **Publication Venue (Original):** *Annalen der Physik und Chemie* (Poggendorff's Annalen)
*   **Volume, Issue, Pages:** Volume 94, Issue 1 (Cumulative Vol. 170), Pages 59–86
*   **URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.18551700105](https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.18551700105)
*   **English Translation Title:** On liquid diffusion
*   **Publication Venue (English):** *The London, Edinburgh, and Dublin Philosophical Magazine and Journal of Science*
*   **Volume, Issue, Pages:** Series 4, Volume 10, Issue 63, Pages 30–39
*   **DOI:** [10.1080/14786445508641925](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786445508641925)
*   **Abstract/Scope:** Establishes the relationship between the flux of a diffusing substance and its concentration gradient (Fick's first and second laws), forming the mathematical foundation for species and consensus diffusion fields.

    *   *Alternative version:* Published as "Sur les lois des mouvements des fluides, en ayant égard à l'adhésion des molécules" in *Annales de Chimie et de Physique*, Vol. 19, pp. 234–245, 1822.
*   **George Gabriel Stokes (1845)**
    *   **Author:** George Gabriel Stokes
    *   **Title:** On the theories of the internal friction of fluids in motion, and of the equilibrium and motion of elastic solids
    *   **Year:** 1845 (Read in 1845; published in 1849)

## 3. Time-Delay Stability

These foundational works analyze linear and transcendental stability boundaries under delayed feedback, supporting the derivations of critical planetary delay-gain boundaries (Theorem 11.3).

### Pontryagin (1942)
*   **Author:** Lev Semenovich Pontryagin
*   **Original Title (Russian):** O nulakh nekotorykh transtsendentnykh funktsiy (О нулях некоторых трансцендентных функций)
*   **Year:** 1942
*   **Publication Venue (Original):** *Izvestiya Akademii Nauk SSSR, Seriya Matematicheskaya* (Bulletin of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR, Mathematics Series)
*   **Volume, Issue, Pages:** Volume 6, Issue 3, Pages 115–134
*   **URL:** [http://www.mathnet.ru/eng/im3121](http://www.mathnet.ru/eng/im3121)
*   **English Translation Title:** On the zeros of some transcendental functions
*   **Publication Venue (English):** *American Mathematical Society Translations*, Series 2
    *   **Volume, Pages:** Volume 1, Pages 95–110
    *   **Year:** 1955
    *   **DOI:** [10.1090/trans2/001/06](https://doi.org/10.1090/trans2/001/06)
*   **Abstract/Scope:** Establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for the zeros of exponential polynomials to lie in the left half of the complex plane, which is the foundational mathematical criterion for determining the asymptotic stability of delay differential equations.

### Bellman & Cooke (1963)
*   **Authors:** Richard Bellman, Kenneth L. Cooke
*   **Title:** Differential-Difference Equations
*   **Year:** 1963
*   **Publisher:** Academic Press, New York
*   **Series/Volume:** Mathematics in Science and Engineering, Volume 6 (462 pages)
*   **URL:** [https://www.rand.org/pubs/reports/R374.html](https://www.rand.org/pubs/reports/R374.html) (RAND Corporation Report R-374-PR) or [https://www.elsevier.com/books/differential-difference-equations/bellman/978-0-12-084950-5](https://www.elsevier.com/books/differential-difference-equations/bellman/978-0-12-084950-5)
*   **Abstract/Scope:** The classic, comprehensive textbook on differential-difference equations, detailing analytical methods, stability theory, asymptotic behavior, and computational techniques for systems with time delays.

## 4. Nyquist Stability Limit

These foundational papers in control theory and information theory define the absolute feedback and sampling bounds for communication networks, forming the basis for the Consensus Latency-Stability Inequality (Theorem 11.4).

### Nyquist (1932)
*   **Author:** Harry Nyquist
*   **Title:** Regeneration Theory
*   **Year:** 1932
*   **Publication Venue:** *Bell System Technical Journal*
*   **Volume, Issue, Pages:** Volume 11, Issue 1, Pages 126–147
*   **DOI:** [10.1002/j.1538-7305.1932.tb02344.x](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1932.tb02344.x)
*   **Abstract/Scope:** Introduces the "Nyquist stability criterion" using loop transfer function frequency response to determine the stability of closed-loop feedback systems, establishing the foundational limits of feedback gain and phase lag.

### Shannon (1948)
*   **Author:** Claude E. Shannon
*   **Title:** A Mathematical Theory of Communication
*   **Year:** 1948
*   **Publication Venue:** *Bell System Technical Journal*
*   **Volume, Issue, Pages:** Volume 27, Issue 3, Pages 379–423 (July 1948) and Issue 4, Pages 623–656 (October 1948)
*   **DOI:** [10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x)
*   **Abstract/Scope:** The founding paper of information theory, establishing fundamental limits on channel capacity, information entropy, and communication sampling rates, which govern the physical latency boundaries for decentralized swarm consensus.
