\begin{abstract}
\noindent
On Genesis Day~2 a five-step plan --- \emph{supply-evidence, clear-obligations,
judge, admit, receipt} --- was written by hand, in PDDL, by a person who knew
the answer. This thesis describes a working prototype,
\texttt{praxis-synthesis}, in which the same plan is \emph{derived}: from
declared capabilities and a saturated fact base, a bounded solver discovers
both the order and the parameter bindings, executes the result as a
content-addressed DAG whose second run replays entirely from cache, and admits
the whole artifact through machine-checkable refinements --- including an
independent replay that does not trust the solver's own bookkeeping. Each of
the four layers is the local instantiation of a published result (Nemo's
scalable Datalog; SMT-discovered capability sequencing; OxyMake's
content-addressable workflows; Flux's refinement discipline), and each rides
on a substrate the host system already owned. We derive the pipeline from
first principles --- the least fixpoint algebra of saturation, the counting
argument for semi-naive evaluation, the search calculus of bounded
sequencing, and the collision-bounded geometry of content addressing --- and
we keep the ledger honest: every claim below is labeled \emph{theorem}
(proved), \emph{measurement} (observed in the test suite), or \emph{refusal}
(deliberately not done, with reasons). The central thesis: \textbf{when
capabilities are declared rather than plans authored, correctness moves from
the author's head into the derivation chain --- and the derivation chain,
unlike the head, can be hashed.}
\end{abstract}

\section{The Problem: Authorship Does Not Scale}

A capability-driven system --- praxis, and by intent any fleet of lawful
agents --- must repeatedly answer one question: \emph{given what is true and
what the system can do, what sequence of actions lawfully reaches the goal?}

There are two ways to answer it.

\paragraph{Authorship.} A person (or an LLM prompted by a person) writes the
plan: a PDDL problem, a workflow file, a runbook. Genesis Day~2 did exactly
this --- the five-step lawobject pipe was hand-encoded, and it was correct
because its author understood the domain. Authorship has a hidden cost
function: it is linear in the number of \emph{plans}, and the number of plans
grows with the product of goals, initial states, and domain variants. A
trillion-agent fleet does not have a trillion authors.

\paragraph{Derivation.} The system is told only what is true (facts), what
follows from truth (rules), and what each capability needs and produces
(declarations). The plan is then a \emph{computed object}, and its correctness
is a property of the derivation procedure --- provable once, reused for every
instance. Derivation's cost is paid in machinery: a reasoner to close the
facts, a solver to discover the sequence, an executor whose outputs can be
audited without rerunning it, and a verifier that does not take the solver's
word for anything.

The prototype under study builds precisely that machinery, under two standing
constraints inherited from the host system's doctrine:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Bounded everything.} Every loop has a cap; every cap violation
  is a first-class refusal carrying salvage data. No panic, no silent
  truncation.
\item \textbf{Receipted everything.} Every stage emits a content address;
  the run composes them into a single hash. Trust attaches to the chain, not
  to the narrative.
\end{enumerate}

Four published results, surfaced by a verified research sweep on 2~July~2026,
supply the blueprint: Nemo demonstrates that Datalog saturation over
$10^5$--$10^8$ facts is a laptop-scale operation \cite{nemo}; the semantic
capability-model literature shows solvers can discover execution orders and
parameter bindings that engineers currently hand-encode \cite{smtcap}; OxyMake
demonstrates content-addressed workflow outputs that make reproducibility a
property of hashes rather than of execution order \cite{oxymake}; and Flux
shows that lightweight refinements --- predicates carried alongside artifacts
--- catch the classes of error that full verification is too expensive to
reach \cite{flux}. The prototype is the composition of the four lessons in
1{,}800 lines of bounded Rust.

\section{Layer 1 --- Saturation: the Algebra of What Follows}

\subsection{The immediate-consequence operator}

Fix a finite alphabet of interned symbols. The prototype inherits its
interning from the host's grounding crate: a dictionary
$\delta : \Sigma \to \{0,\dots,n-1\} \subset \N$ maps strings to dense
\texttt{u32} identifiers, and a ground atom is a pair
$(p, \bar{a})$ with $p$ a predicate id and $\bar{a}$ an argument tuple.
A \emph{database} $\Db$ is a finite set of ground atoms.

\begin{definition}[Rule, safety]
A rule $r$ is $h \leftarrow b_1,\dots,b_k,\ \neg m_1,\dots,\neg m_j$ where
$h, b_i, m_i$ are atoms over variables and constants, subject to the
\emph{safety invariant}: every variable occurring in $h$ or in any $m_i$
occurs in some positive $b_i$. The prototype enforces this at
\texttt{add\_rule} and refuses unsafe rules with a reasoned error.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Immediate consequence]
For a program $P$ (a finite rule set) define
$\Tp : 2^{\mathsf{Atoms}} \to 2^{\mathsf{Atoms}}$ by
\[
\Tp(\Db) \;=\; \Db \,\cup\, \bigl\{\, h\theta \;\bigm|\;
r \in P,\ \theta \text{ grounds } r,\
b_i\theta \in \Db \ \forall i,\
m_i\theta \notin \Db^{-} \ \forall i \,\bigr\},
\]
where $\Db^{-}$ denotes the (already-saturated) database of strictly lower
strata --- the stratification condition defined below.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Existence and finiteness of the fixpoint]
\label{thm:lfp}
For a stratified program over a finite symbol universe, within each stratum
$\Tp$ is monotone on the complete lattice
$(2^{\mathsf{Atoms}},\subseteq)$, hence by Knaster--Tarski has a least
fixpoint $\lfp(\Tp) = \bigcup_{i\ge 0} \Tp^{\,i}(\Db_0)$; and because the
ground-atom universe is finite (at most
$|\mathsf{preds}| \cdot n^{\mathsf{arity}}$ atoms), the ascending chain
stabilizes after finitely many steps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity: within a stratum the negated atoms are evaluated against the
\emph{frozen} lower strata $\Db^{-}$, not against the growing $\Db$, so
enlarging $\Db$ can only enlarge the set of satisfied positive premises.
Knaster--Tarski then gives the least fixpoint as the limit of the ascending
Kleene chain. Finiteness of the universe bounds the chain length by its
cardinality.
\end{proof}

Stratification itself is computed by the standard relaxation: assign
$\mathrm{str}(h) \ge \mathrm{str}(b_i)$ and
$\mathrm{str}(h) > \mathrm{str}(m_i)$, iterate to fixpoint, and refuse
(Section~\ref{sec:refusals}) if the required stratum ever reaches the cap ---
which happens exactly when negation cycles through a predicate's own
definition, the classic \emph{win/lose} pathology, and which the test suite
pins as a receipted \texttt{Unstratifiable} refusal rather than an infinite
loop.

\subsection{Why semi-naive: the counting argument}

The naive Kleene iteration recomputes every join against the \emph{whole}
database each round. The prototype implements \emph{semi-naive} evaluation:
after the first round, each rule is re-joined once per positive body position,
with that position restricted to the tuples derived in the previous round
(the delta).

\begin{proposition}[Semi-naive soundness and completeness]
\label{prop:seminaive}
Every atom derivable by naive iteration is derived by semi-naive iteration,
and conversely.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Soundness is immediate (semi-naive fires a subset of naive instantiations).
Completeness: any \emph{new} derivation at round $i{+}1$ must use at least one
premise derived at round $i$ (else it fired at round $i$ already); the
per-position delta restriction enumerates every choice of which premise is
new. Duplicate firings across positions are harmless because insertion is
idempotent.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Work bound]
\label{prop:work}
Let $\Delta_i$ be the atoms first derived at round $i$ and $F$ the final
database. Naive evaluation performs
$\Theta\!\left(\sum_i |F|^{k}\right)$ pattern probes in the worst case
($k$ the maximal body length), while semi-naive performs
$O\!\left(\sum_i k\,|\Delta_i| \cdot |F|^{\,k-1}\right)$: each round's work is
proportional to \emph{what changed}, not to \emph{what is known}.
\end{proposition}

This is the entire content of the Nemo lesson at prototype scale: saturation
cost must track novelty. The measured witness:

\begin{measurement}[Transitive closure, 100-node chain]
\label{meas:tc}
With $\mathit{edge}$ facts forming a 100-node path and the two-rule
transitive-closure program, saturation derives exactly
$\binom{100}{2} = 4{,}950$ $\mathit{path}$ atoms; the test additionally
asserts multi-round convergence and endpoint reachability
$\mathit{path}(n_0, n_{99})$. The identical program under the host's
backward-chaining kernel is out of reach by construction: that kernel's
derivation depth is capped at $32$, while the chain requires depth~$99$. Test:
\texttt{transitive\_closure\_over\_100\_node\_chain\_saturates}, green.
\end{measurement}

\subsection{The fixpoint as a value: hashing the reasoned state}

Saturation ends by computing
\[
\mathsf{fixpoint\_hash}(\Db)
= \HB\!\Bigl(\;\big\Vert_{\,k \in \mathrm{sort}(K(\Db))}\, k\;\Bigr),
\qquad
K(\Db) = \{\, \mathsf{key}(p,\bar a) \mid (p,\bar a) \in \Db \,\}
\]
where $\mathsf{key}$ is the host's 64-bit atom packing and $\HB$ is BLAKE3.
Sorting makes the hash a function of the \emph{set}, not of derivation order.

\begin{proposition}[Extensional identity]
Two saturations agree on $\mathsf{fixpoint\_hash}$ iff they computed the same
set of packed atom keys (up to $\HB$ collision and 64-bit key collision, both
addressed in Section~\ref{sec:collision}).
\end{proposition}

The consequence is operationally important: \emph{the reasoned state is now a
value}. Layer~2 hashes it into the problem statement; the pipeline hashes it
into the run receipt; a verifier can check that two agents reasoned from the
same closure without exchanging the closure. The test suite pins both
directions --- identical inputs give identical hashes, and one extra fact
changes the hash.

\section{Layer 2 --- Sequencing: Derivation Replaces Authorship}

\subsection{Capabilities as declarations}

\begin{definition}[Capability]
A capability is a tuple
$c = (\mathsf{name},\, k,\, \mathsf{pre},\, \mathsf{add},\, \mathsf{del},\, \mathsf{cost})$
with $k$ parameter variables and pattern-atom lists over those variables,
subject to the same safety invariant as rules: every variable in
$\mathsf{add} \cup \mathsf{del}$ is bound by $\mathsf{pre}$. Violations are
refused at problem construction.
\end{definition}

A \emph{sequencing problem} is $(\Caps, \Db_0, \gamma, H)$: capabilities, the
Layer-1 saturated database as initial state, goal patterns $\gamma$, horizon
$H$. Note what is absent: there is no plan, no ordering, no PDDL text. The
declaration says what each capability \emph{is}; the solver decides what to
\emph{do}.

\subsection{The search calculus and its bounds}

The bundled solver is deterministic branch-and-bound depth-first search over
bound steps:
\[
\text{state} \xrightarrow{\;(c,\theta)\;}
\bigl(\text{state} \setminus \mathsf{del}\,\theta\bigr) \cup \mathsf{add}\,\theta,
\]
where at each depth the candidate bindings $\theta$ are enumerated by
\emph{joining $\mathsf{pre}(c)$ against the current state} --- the same join
algebra as Layer~1. This is the architectural point the research sweep
recommended and the prototype realizes: \textbf{the reasoner's closure is the
solver's binding source.} A capability whose precondition mentions a
\emph{derived} predicate (one produced by rules, never asserted) is
sequencable with no extra machinery, because saturation already materialized
it.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of discovered plans]
\label{prop:sound}
If the solver returns $\Plan = \langle (c_1,\theta_1),\dots,(c_m,\theta_m)\rangle$,
then executing $\Plan$ from $\Db_0$ under the transition relation above
yields a state satisfying $\gamma$, and $m \le H$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The DFS applies exactly the transition relation while descending and restores
state exactly while backtracking (the implementation tracks the precise
added/removed sets per step, so undo is exact even when effects overlap).
A plan is recorded only at a node whose state satisfies the goal join; depth
never exceeds $H$ by the guard.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Optimality within the horizon]
Among plans of length $\le H$ reachable under the caps, the returned plan
minimizes total cost: branch-and-bound prunes only branches whose accumulated
cost already meets or exceeds the incumbent, which cannot exclude a strictly
cheaper completion because costs are non-negative.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Determinism]
\label{prop:det}
Candidate capabilities are tried in declaration order and bindings in the
sorted order of the state's tuple storage; therefore identical problems
produce byte-identical plans and receipts. (Pinned by test:
two full solves serialize identically.)
\end{proposition}

The caps are explicit constants: horizon $\le 16$, bindings per step
$\le 256$, search nodes $\le 10^5$. Exhaustion is not an error state but a
\emph{receipt}: the refusal carries the node count and the best plan found so
far --- salvage, in the doctrine's vocabulary.

\subsection{The flagship derivation}

\begin{measurement}[The lawobject pipe, rediscovered]
\label{meas:flagship}
Declare the five capabilities \emph{supply-evidence} ($\mathit{raw}\,x
\Rightarrow \mathit{evidence}\,x$), \emph{clear-obligations}
($\mathit{evidence}\,x \Rightarrow \mathit{clear}\,x$), \emph{judge}
($\mathit{clear}\,x \Rightarrow \mathit{validated}\,x$), \emph{admit}
($\mathit{validated}\,x \Rightarrow \mathit{admitted}\,x$), \emph{receipt}
($\mathit{admitted}\,x \Rightarrow \mathit{receipted}\,x$), each of cost~1;
assert $\mathit{raw}(o_1)$; goal $\mathit{receipted}(o_1)$; horizon~6.
The solver returns exactly
\[
\langle \text{supply-evidence},\ \text{clear-obligations},\ \text{judge},\
\text{admit},\ \text{receipt} \rangle,
\quad \theta_i = [x \mapsto o_1]\ \forall i,\quad \text{cost } 5,
\]
i.e.\ the order and the binding that Genesis Day~2 hand-authored in PDDL ---
now derived from declarations alone. An independent replay (not the solver's
bookkeeping) re-executes the plan and confirms the goal. Tests:
\texttt{solver\_rediscovers\_the\_five\_step\_lawobject\_order};
unsatisfiable goals and too-short horizons refuse with reasoned
\texttt{Unsatisfiable}; all green.
\end{measurement}

\begin{remark}[What this is and is not]
The example is deliberately small --- a chain, not a lattice. Its value is not
combinatorial difficulty but \emph{provenance}: an artifact that previously
existed only because a human wrote it now exists because a derivation
produced it, and the derivation is hashed into the problem receipt. The
refusal register (Section~\ref{sec:refusals}) is explicit that real SMT
theories --- arithmetic constraints, temporal windows, resource envelopes ---
are the seam left open, behind a trait.
\end{remark}

\section{Layer 3 --- Execution: the Geometry of Content Addressing}

\subsection{From linear plan to DAG}

A discovered plan is a sequence, but its causal structure is sparser. Define
the \emph{data-dependency graph} $\Dag(\Plan)$: node $i$ per bound step, edge
$j \to i$ iff some ground precondition atom of step $i$ was most recently
produced by an add-effect of step $j$ ($j < i$). For the lawobject pipe this
recovers the pure chain (4 edges over 5 nodes --- pinned by test); for two
steps on disjoint objects it produces no edge at all, and the concurrency that
the linear plan hid becomes visible structure.

\begin{proposition}[Acyclicity by construction]
$\Dag(\Plan)$ is acyclic: every edge points from an earlier to a later plan
index. (Hand-built graphs can cycle; the executor's Kahn ordering therefore
re-checks and refuses cycles --- also pinned by test.)
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Execution as hashing}

Execution walks a deterministic topological order (Kahn's algorithm, ties
broken by node identity). For node $v$ with action hash $a_v$ and sorted input
output-hashes $\bar{h}_v$:
\[
\mathsf{key}(v) = \ca(a_v \,\Vert\, \bar h_v), \qquad
\mathsf{out}(v) = \begin{cases}
\text{memo}[\mathsf{key}(v)] & \text{if present (replay)}\\
\text{runner}(v, \text{inputs}) & \text{otherwise (compute, then memoize)}
\end{cases}
\]
and the node receipt folds
$\mathsf{chain} \leftarrow \fold(\mathsf{chain},\,
\{v, a_v, \bar h_v, \HB(\mathsf{out}(v))\})$ --- the host's
$h^{+} = \HB(h^{-} \Vert \mathsf{frame})$ discipline, seeded from a domain
constant.

Two hashes with different jobs close the layer:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the \textbf{chain} (order-sensitive) proves \emph{this execution
  happened in this order and nothing was altered after the fact} --- the test
  suite tampers with one recorded output hash and shows the refold no longer
  matches;
\item the \textbf{root} (order-insensitive),
  $\HB$ over the sorted $(\mathrm{id} : \mathrm{outhash})$ pairs, names
  \emph{the executed artifact set itself}, independent of scheduling.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Reproducibility is content-addressed, not order-addressed]
\label{thm:repro}
If the runner is a function of $(action, inputs)$, then (i) node identity
being content-based (hash of capability, binding, occurrence) makes
$\mathsf{root}$ invariant under any re-declaration order that yields the same
bound steps, and (ii) a second execution against a warm memo cache computes
zero nodes and reproduces the identical root and chain.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof, and its witnesses]
(i) Node ids, edges, keys, and outputs are all functions of step content, and
the root hashes a \emph{sorted} set. Witness: the two-independent-steps test
builds the problem in both declaration orders and asserts equal roots.
(ii) Every memo key present $\Rightarrow$ every node replays; the fold
consumes identical frames. Witness: the warm-cache pipeline test asserts
$\mathsf{replayed} = |V|$ and byte-equal chains. Both green.
\end{proof}

This is the OxyMake lesson in one sentence: \emph{an output is named by what
made it, so recomputation is a cache lookup and reproduction is an equality
check.}

\subsection{Collision honesty}
\label{sec:collision}

All identity claims are ``up to hash collision.'' Two regimes matter.
BLAKE3 at 256 bits: for $N$ hashed objects the collision probability is
$\le N^2/2^{257}$; at a generous $N = 10^{12}$ lifetime objects this is
$< 10^{-53}$ --- ignorable against any physical failure mode. The 64-bit
\emph{atom-key packing} inherited from the host is the honest caveat: at
$N = 10^{5}$ atoms the birthday bound gives collision probability
$\approx N^2/2^{65} \approx 2.7 \times 10^{-10}$; at Nemo-scale
$N = 10^{8}$ it reaches $\approx 2.7 \times 10^{-4}$ and the packing must
widen before the scalability claim transfers. This is recorded as a refusal
(Section~\ref{sec:refusals}), not hidden. These are \emph{estimates}, not
theorems: they assume uniform hashing.

\section{Layer 4 --- Admission: the Verifier Does Not Trust the Solver}

The Flux lesson, transposed from types to artifacts: attach to each artifact a
\emph{refinement} --- a machine-checkable predicate whose evidence travels
with the object. The prototype's verdict runs six refinements with no
short-circuit (every check reports, in the host's \texttt{run\_pipeline}
style):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Refinement & Checks & Independence \\
\midrule
\textsf{PlanRespectsHorizon} & $|\Plan| \le H$ & syntactic \\
\textsf{PlanReachesGoal} & re-executes $\Plan$ from $\Db_0$ & \textbf{does not consult solver state} \\
\textsf{DagAcyclic} & Kahn orders all $|V|$ nodes & structural \\
\textsf{EveryNodeReceipted} & $|\text{receipts}| = |V|$ & structural \\
\textsf{ChainRecomputes} & refolds every frame, byte-compares & \textbf{recomputation, not inspection} \\
\textsf{FixpointClosed} & one more round derives nothing & \textbf{re-runs the reasoner} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{proposition}[The differential stance]
\textsf{PlanReachesGoal} is a second implementation of plan semantics (the
problem's replay path), and \textsf{ChainRecomputes} is a second computation
of the chain. A solver bug that records a wrong plan, or an executor bug that
mis-folds a frame, is caught by disagreement between independent computations
--- the same differential method that caught four real bugs elsewhere in the
host system during Genesis week.
\end{proposition}

Admission is not advisory: \texttt{Synthesis::run} \emph{refuses} the entire
run (with the failed refinement names as salvage) if any check fails. A plan
that cannot survive its own replay does not become an artifact.

\section{The Composition: One Run, One Hash}

The pipeline threads the four layers and folds their stage hashes:
\[
\mathsf{chain}_{\text{run}} = \fold\bigl(\cdots\fold\bigl(\fold\bigl(\fold(g,\;
\mathsf{fixpoint\_hash}),\; \mathsf{plan\_hash}\bigr),\;
\mathsf{root\_hash}\bigr),\; \mathsf{verdict}\bigr)
\]
seeded from the pipeline domain constant $g$. The resulting
\texttt{SynthesisReceipt} is a single value that commits, in order: what was
known (after reasoning), what was decided, what was done, and whether it
survived admission.

\begin{measurement}[End-to-end]
On the lawobject domain: 5 steps discovered, 6/6 refinements pass, the
whole-run chain is a single 64-hex BLAKE3 value; the run is a pure function of
its input (two runs byte-agree on all four stage hashes and the chain); and a
warm-cache rerun replays 100\% of nodes yet produces the \emph{identical}
receipt. Whole crate: \textbf{20/20 tests green, clippy 0 warnings, full
workspace \texttt{--all-features} exit 0.}
\end{measurement}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the Chatman equation]
In the host doctrine's terms $A = \mu(O^{*})$: the admitted observation
$O^{*}$ is here the saturated, hashed fact base; the manufacturing morphism
$\mu$ is the solver-plus-executor; and $R = \mathrm{receipt}(A)$ is the
composed chain. The prototype's specific contribution is to make $\mu$
itself derived --- the morphism is discovered by search over declarations,
not encoded by an author --- while keeping $R$'s discipline intact.
\end{remark}

\section{The Refusal Register}
\label{sec:refusals}

Silent gaps are the only forbidden artifact. What this prototype deliberately
does not do, each with reason and salvage:

\begin{refusal}[No native SMT backend]
Z3/cvc5 are native libraries; linking them breaks the pure-Rust,
forbid-unsafe, deterministic-build doctrine. \emph{Salvage:} the
\textsf{Solver} trait is the seam; \textsf{BoundedCsp} proves the
declare-and-derive architecture with theories deferred, not denied.
\end{refusal}

\begin{refusal}[No Nemo-scale benchmarks]
The join is nested-loop over sorted relations; the claim proved is
architectural (novelty-proportional saturation), demonstrated at
$10^3$--$10^5$ tuples, not $10^8$. \emph{Salvage:} storage is already the
interned sorted-ID form that index joins and worst-case-optimal joins
consume; and the 64-bit atom key must widen first
(Section~\ref{sec:collision}).
\end{refusal}

\begin{refusal}[No parallel execution; no persistent memo cache]
Prototype scope: determinism first. \emph{Salvage:} content addressing
already makes disjoint-input nodes safely parallelizable, and the memo cache
is a plain map behind a stable key scheme.
\end{refusal}

\begin{refusal}[Data edges only in the DAG]
Delete-effects do not order otherwise-independent nodes; a
delete/precondition conflict between concurrent branches is representable but
not yet prevented by an edge. \emph{Salvage:} the discovered plans' linear
order is never violated by \textsf{from\_plan}; conservative interference
edges are a local addition.
\end{refusal}

\begin{refusal}[No CLI verb, no membrane tool]
Surface stability: the API should survive contact before becoming a public
noun. \emph{Salvage:} \texttt{ops::}-mediated wiring is one thin function per
verb, the pattern nine existing nouns already follow.
\end{refusal}

\section{Conclusion: What Moved}

Genesis Day~2's plan was correct because a person understood the domain.
Day~9's identical plan is correct because a fixpoint was proved to exist
(Theorem~\ref{thm:lfp}), a search was proved sound within its caps
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sound}), an execution was made reproducible by
construction (Theorem~\ref{thm:repro}), and an admission layer recomputed
everything it was told (the differential stance) --- with every one of those
claims either proved above, measured in a green test, or refused on the
record.

That is the thesis in one movement: \textbf{authorship placed correctness in
a head; derivation places it in a chain.} Heads do not scale and cannot be
hashed. Chains do, and can.
