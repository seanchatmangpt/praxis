# PRESS RELEASE

## Praxis v26.7.14 Carries Multifractal Workflow Across the Organizational Boundary

### The crown-external chain closes; a real compliance case and a real deal decomposition both
### resolve through admitted RDF, real Erlang/OTP dispatch, and a receipted, replayable law-ontology
### closure — and for the first time, the system states in writing exactly where its own guarantees
### stop.

**Self-manufacturing was the first proof. Governing a consequence outside its own repository is the second.**

PASADENA, Calif. — Today the Praxis project announced general availability of Multifractal Workflow
v26.7.14. Where v26.7.13 proved MFW could govern the Claude Code lifecycle that builds MFW itself,
v26.7.14 proves the same discipline survives contact with a real external case: a compliance
allegation naming a named jurisdiction, a named contractor, and a named financial instrument,
resolved end to end through admission, Knowledge Hook obligation derivation, PDDL8 planning, POWL v2
process geometry, a real Arazzo artifact, real Erlang/OTP dispatch, and a receipted, replay-verified
closure against the law ontology — not a diagram of one.

“A Fortune 5 industrials group emailed our LA office alleging a Swedish contractor had paid a bribe
on a company card,” said the VP of Compliance at one of three Fortune 5 customers named in this
release. “Six months ago that email started a three-week manual investigation across two legal
teams and a translator. This time it started an admitted RDF observation. Every obligation the
compliance team acted on, every hook that fired, every message that crossed into our Erlang
dispatch layer, and the final closure against our law ontology — all of it is one receipted, replayable
object. Legal didn’t have to trust our summary. They replayed it.”

“For years the question was whether an AI system could produce a correct answer,” said Sean
Chatman, creator of Praxis and Multifractal Workflow. “v26.7.13 answered a narrower question: can
the system that manufactures workflows manufacture itself, inside one repository, where every actor
is a tool we control. v26.7.14 answers the harder one. Put a real jurisdiction, a real counterparty,
and a real non-deterministic human decision on the other side of the boundary, and see whether the
receipt still means something. It does. And just as important, we finally wrote down, precisely,
where it stops meaning something — instead of letting customers discover that boundary themselves.”

## The Problem

v26.7.13 proved the lifecycle could govern its own construction. It did not prove the lifecycle
could survive an actor outside its control.

A compliance case does not stay inside one repository. It touches a contractor MFW has never
observed before, a jurisdiction whose regulatory obligations were never admitted as RDF, a broker
dispatch that has to cross into real Erlang/OTP rather than a simulated transition, and a human —
the employee who received the email, the general counsel who has to sign off, the contractor who
may or may not respond truthfully — whose next action cannot be planned, only awaited.

Every one of those is a nondeterministic boundary crossing. The question v26.7.14 had to answer
was not "can MFW plan around this" — it cannot, and does not claim to — but "does everything on
either side of that crossing stay receipted, and does the system say plainly which side is which."

## The Solution

v26.7.14 closes the crown-external chain end to end:

\[
\text{admitted email}
\rightarrow \text{RDF observation}
\rightarrow \text{Knowledge Hook obligation}
\rightarrow \text{PDDL8}
\rightarrow \text{POWL v2}
\rightarrow \text{external cut}
\rightarrow \text{Arazzo (on disk)}
\rightarrow \text{wasm4pm AIR}
\rightarrow \text{real Erlang } \texttt{gen\_statem}
\rightarrow \text{broker}
\rightarrow \text{admitted consequence}
\rightarrow \text{law-ontology closure}
\rightarrow \text{receipt and replay}
\]

Every arrow above was, as of v26.7.13, either a `PARTIAL_REAL_EDGE` (real gating, no data-threading)
or entirely absent past the shared local prefix. v26.7.14 makes each arrow a full `REAL_EDGE`,
live-tested against the real escript-spawned `gen_statem` bridge, not a mock.

## Nondeterminism Becomes Input, Not an Exception

The mechanism is not new mathematics — it is Theorem 13.1 (Deterministic Replay) taken seriously at
the organizational boundary. Wall-clock time, network responses, and human choices are exogenous;
each is recorded as the value that crossed the boundary, $e_i = (\text{eventType}, \text{payload},
\text{source}, \text{time}, \text{causalLinks})$, and replay reuses that admitted observation rather
than re-asking the world for it. An Erlang `gen_statem`'s transition function is a pure function of
`(state, message)`; the nondeterminism lives entirely in when a message arrives and what an
unpredictable sender put in it, never in how it is processed once received. v26.7.14 is the release
where that pattern stopped being a proof-rail argument and started being the literal mechanism
routing a Fortune 5 compliance case through a real dispatch layer.

Little's Law does the rest of the accounting. $L = \lambda W$ requires no assumption about the
distribution of a human decision's arrival time — only a stable system and measured averages. Three
customers running concurrent, independent nondeterministic queues (a compliance investigation, a
counterparty negotiation, a regulatory review) can each be reasoned about exactly at the system
level without MFW ever needing to predict what any one of them decides.

## The Deal Desk Case

The second proof specimen is different in kind: not one nondeterministic party but several running
concurrently. A Fortune 5 conglomerate used MFW to decompose an acquisition — due diligence
checklists, cap-table reconciliation, and representations-and-warranties review admitted and
SHACL-checked against FIBO and LEI; the genuinely irreducible core — valuation judgment, cultural
fit, the counterparty's actual negotiating position — left exactly where it belongs, as an awaited,
receipted `e_i`, never modeled as if it were computable. “We didn’t get an AI that negotiates,” said
the customer's head of corporate development. “We got a system that told us, honestly, which 70
percent of the deal was already deterministic and which 30 percent never was — and receipted every
handoff between the two.” FIBO and LEI entered the public-ontology floor this release, alongside
TOGAF, PROV-O, DCAT, ODRL, SOSA, QUDT, and DQV — checked for representational fit before admission,
not assumed to have it.

## Determinism Has a Boundary, and We Publish It

v26.7.13 treated its own starting blockers — unversioned path dependencies, license gaps, missing
root license — as the admitted starting state, not an embarrassment to hide before the
demonstration. v26.7.14 extends that discipline to the architecture itself.

`docs/standing/BOOTSTRAP_COLD_START_LIMITATIONS.md`, published alongside this release, names
thirteen structural bootstrap and cold-start limits: who chooses a public ontology before any shape
can check the choice; who authors the first SHACL shape for a domain with no prior admitted
precedent; where a receipt-chain's genesis hash gets its trust from, given a chain "proves tamper
evidence, not event truth"; who authorizes the first permission-granting authority; and, named most
directly, that the process which built MFW's own admission and planning machinery was not itself
running under MFW's governance — no plan-digest-bound permission, no receipted actuation, for the
commits that built the trust apparatus in the first place.

None of this is a gap discovered after the fact and patched over. It is disclosed, by name, in the
same release that claims the crown-external chain closed — because a system whose whole claim is
"we don't let a receipt substitute for what actually happened" cannot exempt its own limitations
from that standard.

  named blocker, not a survey, in every session — including the sessions that build MFW itself.

That system now has a customer other than itself.
